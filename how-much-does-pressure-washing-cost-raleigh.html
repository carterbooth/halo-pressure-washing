Factors That Affect the Price

Every property is different, and several variables determine the final cost of a pressure washing job. Understanding these factors helps you evaluate quotes and ensures you're comparing apples to apples when choosing a contractor.

Surface Area (Square Footage)

This is the most straightforward factor. A 1,200 sq ft home costs less to wash than a 3,000 sq ft home. Most contractors measure or estimate square footage to calculate pricing. Larger driveways, decks, and patios cost more than smaller ones.

Surface Type & Material

Vinyl siding cleans differently than brick or stucco. Wood decks require different pressure than composite. Delicate surfaces (like soft wood or painted brick) take longer and require more careful technique, which affects pricing.

Level of Staining or Growth

A lightly dirty driveway is a quick job. A heavily oil-stained driveway with years of buildup requires degreaser, hot water, and multiple passes — all of which add time and cost. Same with heavy algae or moss on roofs and siding.

Accessibility & Site Conditions

Easy access from the street with a water source nearby? Fast and straightforward. Gated property with a long hose run, or a deck on the back of a wooded lot? That adds time. Steep roofs cost more than flat roofs due to safety equipment.

Special Treatments

Standard cleaning is one price. If you need wood brightening before staining, gutter brightening to remove tiger stripes, or sealer/sanding prep, those are add-ons that increase the total cost but deliver better results.

Frequency of Service

One-time cleaning costs more per visit than recurring maintenance. If you schedule quarterly or biannual service, many contractors (including Halo) offer discounted rates because the property stays cleaner and requires less intensive work each visit.
